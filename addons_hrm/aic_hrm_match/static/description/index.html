Staffing Match — who can take this task, and why them

Point it at a task, give it a window and the effort, and it returns a ranked shortlist of the people who can actually do the work. Every number on that list opens the record it came from.

  • Standalone. Community dependencies only: Employees, Skills, Project
  • Every score is explainable — and links to the task, leave or project behind it
  • Nobody is dropped silently — every exclusion keeps the reason that produced it
  • Starts from the task you already have, no parallel planning system

Picking people from memory does not scale

With twelve people, the delivery lead knows who is free and who has done this before. With ninety, they know the same twelve. The rest of the bench is invisible, the same names get loaded until they leave, and the person who did the last three projects for this exact customer is sitting idle two floors away.

The failure is not that the choice is wrong. It is that nobody can say why it was made, so it cannot be reviewed, improved, or defended when a customer asks.

From a task to a ranked shortlist in two clicks

Open a project task, press Find Best Fit, confirm the window and the effort. The request is a record, not a popup: it keeps the ranking, the parameters it ran under, and the decision that followed.

Twelve criteria you can read, change and defend — seven on by default

Availability, skill fit, certifications, similar projects, customer history, seniority fit and workload balance are enabled out of the box. Domain experience, cost fit, location, timezone overlap and language are one switch away.

Hard constraints eliminate; soft criteria rank. Somebody with no free hours in the window is not scored low, they are excluded, with the four overlapping bookings listed. A required certificate that expires before the work ends is a gate, not a deduction.

Every number carries its evidence

The score is a link, not an opinion. Open any candidate and each criterion shows the raw value, the normalised score, the weight, and the contribution — with the records underneath it. “0.80 customer history” expands to four engagements with this customer, most recent first.

Change the weights, see what changes — before you save

Scoring policies are versioned. An active one is locked; to change it you fork a new version, replay it against a real past request, and read the rank differences side by side before activating. Adjusting how people are chosen is a decision with a record, not an edit somebody made on a Tuesday.

The decision is a record, and the overrides teach the system

Choosing somebody other than the top match asks for a category and a reason. Those overrides become a report: when “the criterion is wrong” keeps appearing for the same criterion, that is the weights telling you they need changing.

Privacy is a setting, not a promise

Employees never see each other's scores. Performance figures sit behind their own access group, so a planner can rank a shortlist without being shown anyone's appraisal results. Blind ranking is available: names are replaced by references until the decision is made, while the audit record always keeps the real identity.
